The summer I sold televisions at Costco, the same season I sat on an autonomous-vehicle engineering team, I learned one lesson in two uniforms. The customer is a funnel. The worker is a cost. The product is whoever writes the spec.

I am a University of Florida computer science student with a minor in artificial intelligence. I tutor. I write code. I work campus IT when machines break. This fall I published a book under the name Julian King: Arguments for An Abolishment of Corporate-Owned America. It is a political critique, not a street manual. Abolishment, in the title, means the dissolution of corporate rule over the wage, the news, the lease, the clinic, and the naming of wars. Replacement by the people who do the work. Not wreckage of persons.

Gainesville is a good laboratory. Rent is a corporate product. The dining hall is a corporate product. The phone that wakes a student is a corporate product that also trains a model on the fact that the student woke. If a war widens, the same companies will sell the footage and the explanation. We will be asked to ratify a piece. Nobody will be asked to vote on the whole.

I am not asking campus to burn anything. I wrote under Camus's limit: refuse the costume of necessity. A third world war will not arrive as a newsreel. It will arrive as a regional fire that several nuclear patrons cannot afford, domestically, to be the first to put out. Students are useful in that story. We are young enough to be drafted into the explanation and old enough to be billed for the rest.

What I want from a university town is smaller and harder than a rally. Stop pretending the corporate form is weather. It is a charter: a permission slip to concentrate capital, limit liability, and outlive the people who signed it. Charters can be rewritten. Workers' councils are not a Halloween costume. They are a way of saying the people who do the work set the terms of the work.

I have no follower count to impress you with. I have a manuscript, a Venmo, and a student ID. If you want the long version, it is a digital book and the Author's Note is free. I will not sell it in this newspaper. I will only say that I wrote it here, as a student, because the alternative was to keep the argument in a folder while the funnel kept running.
