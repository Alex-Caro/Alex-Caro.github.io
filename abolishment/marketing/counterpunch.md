# CounterPunch submission

To: submissions@counterpunch.org
Subject: Web piece, 900 words: The charter is not weather

Please consider this essay for the website. About 900 words. Original. Peaceful political critique. Not a call to violence.

I am Alexander Caro, writing as Julian King. UF computer science student. The book this is drawn from is Arguments for An Abolishment of Corporate-Owned America, digital, 2026.

If you cannot use it, no need to reply.

---ESSAY---

The corporate form is treated as climate. It is a charter.

A charter is a permission slip from a state to concentrate capital, limit liability, and outlive the people who signed it. The Tea Act was a bailout dressed as a tax cut: a favored company writing the rules of a game it also played. The harbor is only the first photograph. The photograph we live inside is the 250-year American empire, late, armed, and convinced that its exceptionalism is a physical law.

I do not treat Glubb's cluster around two centuries as physics. I treat it as a rhyme the present is living inside. Empires in that window do not fall because a student said so. They strain. They rename the strain. They sell the renaming as strategy, then as necessity, then as the only adult position in the room.

Abolishment, as I am using it, is an institutional word. Dissolution of corporate rule over the wage, the news, the lease, the medicine, and the slope of a war. Replacement by workers' councils and federated production. Not wreckage of persons. Not a night of scores. Camus's limit sits on every page I published: refuse the costume of necessity. Diagnosis is not a recipe. A pamphlet that forgets the difference becomes the thing it claims to oppose.

The next general war will not announce itself. It will arrive as a regional fire that several nuclear patrons cannot afford, domestically, to be the first to put out. Security guarantees sold in peacetime as limits become transmission belts in a week. Nobody voted for the whole. Everybody is asked to ratify a piece. Students are useful in that story. We are billed for the rest.

I spent a summer selling high-ticket electronics and another stretch on an autonomous-vehicle engineering team. Same lesson in two uniforms. The customer is a funnel. The worker is a cost. The product is whoever writes the spec. Surveillance is not a subplot. It is how the spec gets written in 2026. The phone that wakes you also trains a model on the fact that you woke.

If this sounds like a campus pamphlet, good. Pamphlets are how charters get argued in public. The long version is a digital book. I am not asking CounterPunch to sell it. I am asking whether the charter-as-weather story still deserves the space it occupies, and whether abolishment can be spoken as dissolution rather than wreckage.

Julian King

---END ESSAY---
